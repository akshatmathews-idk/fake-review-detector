01 / THE QUESTION

What exactly are we trying to predict?

We frame the task as supervised binary classification: given the review text, predict the dataset label.

“

Can the words and short phrases in a review provide useful signals for distinguishing the dataset’s CG and OR labels?

The model receives only the review text. Ratings and product categories are retained for exploratory and error analysis, not used as prediction-time inputs.

“Original” and “computer-generated” refer to the labels supplied by this dataset. The model learns associations with those labels; it does not verify who wrote a review or whether its claims are true.

03 / DATA PREPARATION

Clean first. Split second. Learn within folds.

Preprocessing was deliberately limited: preserve review wording while preventing repeated texts from leaking across the split.

01
Trim whitespace

Leading and trailing whitespace was stripped from review text.

02
Validate records

Missing text, missing labels, empty reviews and conflicting labels for duplicate normalised texts were checked. No missing or empty review texts and no conflicting duplicate labels were found.

03
Remove repeated texts

22 duplicate text occurrences were removed after whitespace normalisation, retaining the first occurrence of each unique text.

04
Encode and split

OR → 0 and CG → 1. A stratified 80/20 split used random_state=42.

TRAINING SET32,32816,172 OR · 16,156 CG
HELD-OUT TEST SET8,0824,043 OR · 4,039 CG

Leakage control: TF–IDF was placed inside each scikit-learn pipeline. During cross-validation, vocabulary and IDF weights were fitted only on that fold’s training portion. Duplicate review texts were removed before splitting, and no train–test text overlap remained.

FEATURE REPRESENTATION

Why TF–IDF?

TF–IDF gives weight to terms based on their frequency in a review and how common they are across the document collection. Unigrams represent one word; bigrams represent consecutive two-word phrases.

1–2 gramsWords + adjacent pairs
min_df=2Discard terms in fewer than 2 documents
max_df=0.95Ignore terms in over 95% of documents
sublinear_tf=TrueUse 1 + log(term frequency)
05 / EXPERIMENTS

Model choice was empirical, not assumed.

We used stratified five-fold cross-validation on the training set. Each fold acted once as validation data while the other four folds were used for fitting.

09 / LIMITATIONS

What should we not claim?

Strong results on one labelled dataset do not automatically transfer to other review sources or real-world settings.

01Dataset dependence

The model learns patterns in this particular dataset. Performance may change across platforms, product types or time periods.

02Label validity

The dataset’s CG/OR labels may not perfectly represent the real-world distinction between generated and original writing.

03Limited representation

TF–IDF captures word and short-phrase statistics, not deep context, intent, sarcasm or factual accuracy.

04Evaluation caveat

The held-out test set was inspected during development before feature experiments were complete.

05Not a fraud detector

The classifier cannot establish deception, fraud, authenticity or the identity of an author.

06No significance test

Observed CV improvements were not tested for statistical significance.

TAKEAWAY

Text patterns provide useful signals for reproducing the labels in this dataset.

LinearSVC with TF–IDF unigrams and bigrams, min_df=2, max_df=0.95 and sublinear term frequency performed best among the configurations tested. The result is promising within the study’s scope, but it is not evidence of a universal AI-review detector.
